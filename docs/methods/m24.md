## Summary

**Negative result on quality.** On all 40 uc24 test instances the m24 pipeline (5-member temporal-GNN ensemble on 900
instances, guard-aware Learning to Fix at ε = 1 %, LP-relaxation guard, reduced MILP at 0.5 %) is **1.65× faster** than
learning-free Learning to Fix on the LP relaxation at the same 0.5 % gap. But it is **less accurate**: the mean gap is
**0.57 % vs 0.34 %** (paired Δ +0.24 pp, 95 % CI [+0.10, +0.41]). The tail is heavier too: **8 of 40 instances
above 1 %**, max 2.37 %, against 2 of 40 and a max of 1.28 % (LtF-LP also has 1 infeasible reduced problem; the
pipeline has none). It fixes 91.1 % of the decisions, against 80.6 %. By time to the same quality, the pipeline gains
3.4× (geometric mean) on the full MILP, against 2.6× for LtF-LP and 1.4–2.3× for the earlier 24 h rules. Its edge over
LtF-LP by that measure, 1.34×, is not significant (CI [0.99, 1.80]).

So the recipe improves on every earlier *learned* 24 h rule. It is as accurate as the guarded 95 % rule and LtF-kNN or
better, and 1.2–2.8× faster than them. It still does not beat the learning-free baseline: the extra ten points of
fixings buy speed and cost quality. The guard-aware tuning itself now converges on 24 hours: 91.6 % fixed on
validation, with the guards releasing only 0.1 pp. That convergence comes from the minimal min up/down conflict
release. But the validated ε does not carry over to test: 6 of 40 test instances exceed a 1 % cost increase over the
full MILP at the 0.1 % reduced gap, against 0 of 40 on validation by construction. Validation alone would have shown this. In a
two-fold cross-validation on the 40 validation instances, thresholds tuned on 20 instances and applied to the other 20
violate ε on **11 of 40** held-out cases for m24 (max 4.67 %), against 3 of 40 for LtF-LP (one infeasible, max
1.62 %). In-sample, both are 0 of 40.

**Follow-up** (section below): a one-nat logit margin on the thresholds, chosen by that cross-validation (2 of 40 held-out
violations), removes the tail. On a fully paired re-solve of the test set the calibrated rule has 0.29 % mean gap and
none of 40 instances above 1 % (max 0.95 %). That is level with LtF-LP (−0.06 pp, CI across 0), 1.14× faster in raw
time (not significant) and 1.38× faster at the same quality (CI [1.03, 1.89]). The cguard bound check with a full-MILP
fallback removes most of the original rule's tail (8 → 2 instances above 1 %), but it fires on 18 % of the test
instances, against 2.5 % on validation, and costs a quarter of the speed. On the calibrated rule it adds little.

## Method

**Question.** On 12 hours the recipe of [`m1x.md`](m1x.md) — more training instances with teacher-polished labels, a
temporal-GNN ensemble, Learning to Fix's joint ε-tuning on its probabilities with guards inside the check, the
LP-relaxation guard at test and a reduced MILP at a 0.5 % gap — fixes more decisions at the same validated ε and gives
the hybrid's gap at a higher speed-up. On 24 hours no learned rule has beaten the learning-free baseline yet:
Learning to Fix's tuning on the LP-relaxation values with the reduced MILP at 0.5 % gives 0.35 % at 15.7× ([`base.md`](base.md)
§4.4, first 20 test instances). Does the recipe change that?

**1. Data without full MILPs.** uc24 ([`b3.md`](b3.md)) has 300 training instances (LP relaxation and label-free labels
only), 120 of which got 0.5 %-gap MILP schedules for the kNN of [`uc24ltf.md`](uc24ltf.md), 40 validation instances with
full MILPs (`uc24ltf_val.npz`) and 40 test instances. New training-day scenarios are drawn with the generator of
`train.npz` (`otsl.b3.make_jobs` / `instance`, mode "lp", seed 404 instead of 101). Labels:

* the 120 MILP-labelled instances keep their schedules;
* every other training instance (180 original + the new ones) gets a **teacher-polished label**: the teacher (3
  temporal GNNs trained on the 120 MILP labels) fixes its 90 % most confident decisions, the adequacy guard, the
  minimal min up/down conflict release (below) and the LP-relaxation guard release what they would release at test,
  and a 10 s reduced MILP (0.1 %) fills in the rest; the schedule is kept if the dispatch LP prices it below the
  block-repaired label-free label. No full MILP is solved. The 10 s limit was chosen by a pilot on training instances
  100–119 with a teacher trained on the other 100 MILP-labelled instances.

**2. Model.** The temporal GNN of `otsl.m1x.CommitGNNT` (GNN + per-hour residual dilated 1-D convolutions) with dilations
1, 2, 4, 8 so that every hour of the 24-hour row sees every other; 5 seeds, mean probability. Training as in m1x (BCE on
canonical labels, AdamW 1e-3, batch 64, cosine schedule, early stopping on the validation log-loss).

**3. Minimal conflict release** (`otsl.m24.release_min_conflicts`). On 24 hours a rounded row is often not min
up/down-feasible. The 12-hour row release frees the whole row; inside the tuning check that makes the master collapse
every interval (uc24ltf.md: 100 % fixed before the guards, 42 % after), and leaving conflicts to the cuts (plain LtF)
did not converge on GNN probabilities. Here a dynamic program finds, per conflicting row, a min up/down-feasible
schedule that disagrees with the fewest fixed entries (free entries cost nothing), and only those fixings are
released (on a 3 %-noisy rounding: ~35 released instead of ~600 per instance, no conflict left).

**4. Tuning** (`otsl.m24.M24Tuner` = `uc24ltf.GuardedLtFTuner` with this guard chain): Learning to Fix's Algorithm 1 + 2
with Appendix A's objective, ε = 1 %, on the 40 validation instances; inside the check, the cuts and the verification
the fixings pass through the adequacy guard and the minimal conflict release. Settings of the uc24ltf runs (Q = 20,
K_max = 10, relaxation MILPs 8 s, check MILPs 60 s, warm-start candidates).

**5. Pipeline at test**: ensemble probabilities → eq. (5) with the tuned thresholds → adequacy guard → minimal conflict
release → LP-relaxation guard → reduced MILP at mip_rel_gap 0.5 % (and, to separate the two effects, at 0.1 %).

**6. Validation-only diagnostic** (`scripts/uc_m24_cv.py`, run after the test): does the validated ε hold on
instances the tuning did not see? Two-fold cross-validation on the 40 validation instances (fixed random split, seed
0): the thresholds of each score (ensemble with guards; LP relaxation without guards, as base) are tuned with the same
settings on 20 instances and applied, with the test-time guards, to the other 20; the reduced MILP (0.1 %, 120 s) is
compared with the instance's full-MILP objective C*. In-sample control: the 40-instance thresholds on the same 40
instances, measured the same way. This uses no test data; it was added after the test showed a heavy tail, to see
whether validation alone would have shown it.

## Setup

* **Compute**: core 1 only, HiGHS (highspy 1.15, as in uc24ltf / base) and PyTorch single-threaded, one job at a time
  (`scripts/uc_m24_queue.sh`).
* **Selection**: validation only (polishing setting on training instances; models, thresholds on the 40 validation
  instances). The test set is read once, by `scripts/uc_m24_eval.py`.
* **Test protocol**: all 40 uc24 test instances; per instance, in one process: LP relaxation (timed), full MILP at
  0.1 % / 300 s with its incumbent log (dual bound DB, speed-up denominator, time-to-quality reference), full MILP at
  0.5 %, learning-free LtF on the LP relaxation at 0.5 % (base's thresholds `base_tune_lp_1.json`, tuned on the same
  40 validation instances), the earlier guarded 95 % rule at 0.1 % (published setting) and 0.5 %, and the m24 rule at
  0.1 % and 0.5 % (the pipeline). Method time = inference (all 5 members) + LP relaxation + fixing + guards + reduced
  MILP. No fallback for infeasible reduced problems.
* **Metrics**: paper metrics (gap to DB, feasibility, mean / median / geometric-mean speed-up), served share, fixed
  share; time to the same quality (first time the full MILP's incumbent reaches the method's cost, censored at its
  termination) divided by the method's time; paired instance-bootstrap 95 % CIs.

## Verdict

* **Does the recipe beat the learning-free baseline on 24 hours? No.** The pipeline is 1.65× faster than LtF-LP at the
  same 0.5 % gap but 0.24 pp worse (CI [+0.10, +0.41]), with four times as many instances above 1 % (8 vs 2) and nearly
  twice the worst case (2.37 % vs 1.28 %). It is a different point on the speed/quality curve, not a better one. By time
  to the same quality its advantage (1.34×) is not significant.
* **Against the earlier learned 24 h rules it is the best so far.** Its quality matches or beats the guarded 95 % rule
  and LtF-kNN, it is 1.2–2.8× faster, and its TTQ speed-up over the full MILP (3.4× geometric mean) is the highest of
  any 24 h method measured, LtF-LP included (2.6×).
* **What works:** polished labels (a 10 s reduced MILP lands within 0.04 % of the 0.5 %-gap MILP at a fraction of its
  cost) and data scaling (log-loss 0.065 → 0.058). The minimal min up/down conflict release makes guard-aware Learning to
  Fix converge on 24 hours with the guards barely changing the fixed share.
* **What does not:** turning the better score into safe extra fixings. The tuning fixes 11 pp more than LtF-LP at the
  same validated ε, but on test those fixings cost 0.2 pp on average and push 6 of 40 instances past the ε that held on
  every validation instance. The learned score makes no fewer outright errors than rounding the LP relaxation (27.5 vs
  25.5 per validation instance), so the extra fixings are not safer than LtF-LP's own.
* **Validation could have caught this.** In-sample feasibility on 40 instances is not enough evidence for a learned
  score: the two-fold cross-validation shows 11 of 40 held-out ε violations for m24 against 3 of 40 for LtF-LP. The
  next step, before the test set is used again, is to choose the operating point by held-out violation rate on
  validation rather than by in-sample feasibility. Examples: a smaller ε, or a safety margin on the thresholds, picked
  so the cross-validated violation rate matches LtF-LP's. Then check whether the speed advantage survives. Not run
  here.
* **Follow-up (below):** exactly that calibration, a one-nat logit margin chosen by the cross-validation, gives the
  first learned 24-hour rule that is level with LtF-LP. Mean gap 0.29 % vs 0.35 %, no instance above 1 %, 1.14× faster
  in raw time (not significant), 1.38× faster at the same quality (significant). The cguard bound check is the weaker fix
  on 24 hours.

## Follow-up: calibrated thresholds and the cost-aware bound check

`scripts/uc_m24_fu.py`, cores 0 and 1 (one single-threaded process per core). Tables:
[`results/uc24/m24_results.md`](../../results/uc24/m24_results.md), section *Follow-up*. Every choice was made on
validation, but the follow-up itself was prompted by the test result above, and the test set was used once more.

**1. Calibration by the two-fold cross-validation.** The goal was to bring m24's held-out ε-violation rate down to
LtF-LP's (3 of 40) or lower with the smallest change. Two families were tried, each from its smallest change upwards:

* a margin on the tuned thresholds in logit space, lo' = σ(logit(lo) − δ), hi' = σ(logit(hi) + δ);
* a tighter ε in the tuning.

Within each family the first setting that met the target was taken; across the families, the one that kept more
fixings on the held-out instances.

| m24 thresholds (2-fold CV, held out) | cases above 1 % | fixed |
|---|---|---|
| tuned at ε = 1 % (unchanged) | 11 / 40 | 94.2 % |
| margin δ = 0.5 | 7 / 40 | 92.8 % |
| **margin δ = 1 (chosen)** | **2 / 40** | 90.2 % |
| tuned at ε = 0.5 % | 4 / 40 | 93.3 % |
| tuned at ε = 0.25 % | 6 / 40 | 91.9 % |
| LtF-LP (reference) | 3 / 40 | 79.0 % |

**A tighter ε does not control the out-of-sample risk; a margin does.** Halving ε twice still leaves 4–6 held-out
violations. The thresholds still sit at the new edge, and that edge is just as specific to the instances the tuning saw.
Moving every threshold outwards by one nat removes the decisions that are only barely confident. The margin was
applied to the 40-instance thresholds (`m24_tune_hg_fin.json`), with no new tuning. That fixes 85.7 % of the validation
decisions and 85.0 % on test, against 91.6 % / 91.1 % without the margin and 80.5 % / 80.6 % for LtF-LP. A 40-instance
tuning at ε = 0.25 % (`m24_tune_hg_fin_e2.5.json`) was run speculatively while the cross-validation ran and is unused.

**2. The bound check of the cguard study (C3).** g = (C_red − z_LP) / C_red against the LP relaxation solved as input.
If g > d, or the reduced problem is infeasible, the full MILP at 0.5 % is solved and the better solution kept. d is set
per rule as the smallest value of cguard's grid that fires on at most 5 % of the 40 validation instances, using the
rule's reduced MILP at 0.5 %. The grid is 1–10 %, extended down to 0.5 % because 24-hour relaxation gaps are small
(median 0.26 %), but the extension was not needed. Result: d = 1 % for LtF-LP, 1.5 % for m24, 1 % for the calibrated
rule; each fires on 2.5 % of validation.

**3. Test, fully paired re-solve.** A first pass on core 1 re-solved the original m24 pipeline next to the calibrated
one. It reproduced the stored objectives on 40 of 40, but took 1.35–1.65× the stored time on instances 0–25 and
0.9–1.0× on 26–39. Instances 0–25 had been run before a container restart, evidently on a faster machine; the
original test is unaffected, because every ratio there is within one process. The stored full-MILP times therefore

* **The calibrated rule removes the tail at no loss of quality.** None of the 40 test instances is above 1 % (max
  0.95 %, against 2.37 % before and 1.36 % for LtF-LP). The mean gap is level with LtF-LP (−0.06 pp, CI across 0) and
  with the full MILP at 0.5 % (+0.03 pp). It is 1.14× faster than LtF-LP in raw time, which is not significant, but
  **1.38× faster at the same quality** (TTQ, CI [1.03, 1.89]). On each of the original rule's eight tail instances
  the calibrated rule lands at 0.16–0.95 %. It is the first learned 24-hour rule that is not worse than the
  learning-free baseline. It is not clearly better either: the speed gain at equal quality is real but small, and it
  rests on one test pass.
* **The bound check removes most of the original rule's tail, at a speed cost.** It fires on 7 of the 8 tail
  instances; it misses instance 22 (g = 1.40 % < 1.5 %), and on instance 7 the full MILP at 0.5 % is itself at
  1.01 %. That brings the mean gap from 0.57 % to 0.39 % and the max from 2.37 % to 1.30 %, but costs about a quarter
  of the speed (geometric mean 11.0× → 8.3×). The check fires on 18 % of the test instances against 2.5 % on
  validation, because the thresholds were picked on in-sample reduced solutions, whose g is smaller. The result is still
  0.13 pp worse than the full MILP at 0.5 % and no better than LtF-LP. On LtF-LP the check fires on 20 % (including
  the infeasible instance), improves the gap by 0.08 pp and costs 24 % of the time. On the calibrated rule it fires on
  12 % and changes little (−0.02 pp, 16 % slower).
* **Answer to "does the check remove the tail at an acceptable speed cost?"** Mostly, but calibrating the thresholds
  removes the tail more completely and costs less. On 24 hours the risk sits in the thresholds; the check repairs
  solutions after the fact and fires far more often out of sample than on validation.
* **Caveats.** One test pass after a second look at the test set. The calibration target (LtF-LP's held-out rate) and
  both families were set before the calibration runs, and every choice used validation only, but the follow-up was
  prompted by the first test result. Speed-ups here are on the slower machine, so they are lower in absolute terms than
  in the main table. Two processes ran side by side on cores 0 and 1 (load about 2), and each instance's rules shared
  one process.
* **Compute** over cores 0 and 1: cross-validation 1.8 h, validation runs 0.5 h, the unused
  ε = 0.25 % tuning 0.3 h, the first test pass 0.35 h and the paired re-solve 3.0 h; about 6 core-hours in all.

## Caveats

* **One test run of 40 instances.** The CIs are instance-bootstrap CIs over those 40. The test was interrupted by
  container restarts: instances 0–25 ran from 10:04 to 11:48 and instances 26–39 from 15:38 to 16:58, in a fresh
  process. Each instance's rules ran back to back in one process (paired timing), and the load average was about 4 in
  both periods (other agents on other cores), so the ratios are comparable. Instance 26 was started three times before
  and killed by the restarts; only the complete run is recorded.
* **LtF-LP** uses base's thresholds (`base_tune_lp_1.json`, the same 40 validation instances and settings), faithful
  without guards and without fallback: one infeasible reduced problem (test 9) counts as a failure. It is excluded from
  the paired Δs (n = 39) and lowers its feasible and served shares.
* **LtF-kNN** is the stored uc24ltf run: its speed-up is against that run's full-MILP time, so it is not paired with
  this run's timing.
* **Served** is strict (any reserve shortfall above 1e-6 MW). Every miss in this run is a reserve shortfall of at most
  0.045 MW, the full MILP included.
* **Labels.** The polished labels come from a 10 s reduced MILP that stopped at its limit on 60 % of instances; 10 % of
  the labels fall back to the block-repaired label-free label. The teacher was trained on the 120 MILP-labelled
  instances only, with no further rounds of self-training.
* **The ε guarantee is in-sample.** Learning to Fix guarantees ε on the instances it is tuned on. With a score that
  fixes more, the thresholds sit closer to the edge for more decisions, and the guarantee carries less out of sample.
  The 0.1 % / 0.5 % reduced gaps add to the cost increase measured on test.
* **Compute** (core 1): new scenarios 0.26 h, polished labels 1.87 h plus the pilot (0.1 h), training 0.75 h (all
  models), tuning 0.27 h, test 3.1 h (plus the partial runs of instance 26 killed by restarts), cross-validation
  diagnostic 1.8 h. Total about 8.1 h, within the 10–12 h budget.
