# B3: a 24-hour unit-commitment benchmark where the full MILP takes minutes

*Code: `otsl/b3.py`, `scripts/uc_b3_probe.py`, `uc_b3_gen.py`, `uc_b3_train.py`, `uc_b3_fix.py`, `uc_b3_report.py`,
`uc_b3_chain.sh`. Results: `results/uc24/b3_results.md` (+ `.json`), logs `results/uc24/b3_*.log`.*

## 1. Why

On B2 (12 hours) the full MILP takes ~30 s, which caps measurable speed-ups (RESEARCH.md §5, W4). Learning to Fix
reports 0.48 % gap at 20.8× on EPRI's competition systems. B3 asks whether the label-free pipelines (imitation of the
repaired LP relaxation → REINFORCE with the dispatch LP as critic → block adequacy repair / guarded partial fixing)
give large speed-ups on instances where the MILP takes minutes, while training without any full MILP.

## 2. Benchmark definition (`uc24`)

Same system and model as B1/B2 (`otsl/uc.py`: RTS-GMLC, 73 thermal units, DC network with 120 branches, min up/down,
ramping, start-up costs, 3-segment costs, 3 % spinning reserve, VOLL-priced shedding / over-generation and priced
reserve shortfall), with

* **horizon T = 24 hours, start hour 0** (a full calendar day), network on;
* scenario sampling of `otsl.uc.make_scenario` (area load × U[0.92, 1.08], bus load × U[0.97, 1.03], renewables ×
  U[0.8, 1.2], initial status = priority list of the hour before the window); day splits of `scripts/uc_gen.py`
  (test = day % 5 == 0, val = day % 5 == 2, train = the rest);
* **full MILP: HiGHS, 0.1 % relative gap, 300 s time limit, one thread per solve, two solves in parallel.**
  The model is exactly `UCModel` (5,256 binaries; 22,344 variables; 16,344 rows); it is passed to `highspy`
  1.15.1 directly instead of `scipy.optimize.milp` (HiGHS 1.12) so that every improving incumbent is logged
  (`otsl.b3.solve_milp_hs`). All B3 MILPs, full and reduced, use this path. The dispatch LP of the MILP's commitment
  reproduces the MILP objective (max relative difference ~1e-12).

T = 36 was not tried: `make_scenario` only supports windows inside one calendar day, and T = 24 is already in the
target regime.

### Probe (4 training-split days, 900 s limit, 0.1 % gap; `results/uc24/b3_probe_T24.json`)

| day | stop | final gap | gap at 60 / 120 / 300 / 600 s | incumbent within 0.02 % of final at | LP-relaxation gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 (Jan) | 900 s limit | 0.225 % | 2.83 / 0.43 / 0.31 / 0.26 % | 73 s | 0.81 % |
| 108 (Apr) | 900 s limit | 0.142 % | 0.36 / 0.22 / 0.16 / 0.14 % | 19 s | 0.35 % |
| 196 (Jul) | optimal (0.1 %) at 80 s | 0.089 % | 0.28 % / done | 74 s | 0.16 % |
| 288 (Oct) | optimal (0.1 %) at 75 s | 0.096 % | 0.86 % / done | 75 s | 0.16 % |

Measured on a shared 4-core machine (1-minute load average 8–9 during the probe; on the test MILPs, with load
average 6.7, CPU time was 0.70 of wall time). **Choice: 0.1 % gap, 300 s limit.** On the 40 test
instances the full MILP then takes 154 s on average (median 98 s, range 3–300 s); 30 % stop at the limit with final
gaps of 0.10–0.77 %, the others prove 0.1 % (§4.1). A 600–900 s limit would cost 2–3× the solver time for a
0.05–0.1 pp better bound on the hard instances; the generation budget (40 test MILPs, 2 workers) did not allow it.

**Important property seen in the probe**: the full MILP finds an incumbent within 0.02 % of its final one after
20–75 s; the remaining minutes are spent on the dual bound. Speed-ups measured against the MILP's termination time
and against its time-to-quality are therefore very different, and both are reported.

## 3. Protocol: no full MILP for training or selection

| split | instances | content | full MILP |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | 300 | scenario, LP relaxation (commitment, LMPs, flows), two repaired relaxation labels with their dispatch-LP costs | **none** (u = −1, obj = NaN) |
| val | 30 | same as train | **none** — all selection is label-free (below) |
| test | 40 | same + full MILP (300 s, 0.1 %) with final gap, dual bound, optimality flag, incumbent log, CPU time | yes |

*Deviation from the plan (val with full MILPs).* The probe showed that each full MILP costs 1.5–5 min of a worker and
that the fixing evaluation (six reduced MILPs per test instance plus back-to-back re-solves) needs a similar budget, so
the val full MILPs were dropped and **every selection on val is made against the val LP-relaxation bound** (mean
dispatch-LP cost of a decoded commitment divided by the instance's LP-relaxation cost; for fixing, the mean cost of
the reduced MILP's solution relative to the same bound). This is a valid ranking criterion (the per-instance
reference is a constant for every candidate) and keeps the full pipeline — training *and* model selection — free of
full MILPs.

Pipelines (all reuse `otsl/ucml.py`, `otsl/constrained.py`, `otsl/fixpolicy.py`, `scripts/uc_fixing.py` by import):

1. **Imitation (LF-BCE)**: GNN with LP-relaxation features (`UCFeaturizer(relax=True, sym=True)`, `build_uc_model1`),
   BCE on a repaired relaxation label, 60 epochs, early stopping on val BCE against the same label-free targets. Two
   labels were trained and the better one on val kept: `y_lf` = rounded relaxation + per-hour adequacy repair +
   min up/down repair (the B2 label, V4) and `y_blk` = rounded relaxation + block adequacy repair + min up/down repair
   (W1).
2. **REINFORCE with the dispatch-LP critic** (`constrained.train_reinforce_plain`): reward −log(LP cost / LP-relaxation
   cost), leave-one-out baseline over 6 samples, 12 instances per step, min up/down repair of every sample, 80 steps;
   checkpoints every 20 steps, the one with the lowest mean val cost (top-1 + block repair) kept.
3. **End-to-end decoding**: threshold (0.5 or chosen on val from 0.3–0.9) → block adequacy repair → min up/down
   repair → one dispatch LP; and candidate screening (7 thresholds + 8 samples, each block-repaired, best by LP).
4. **Partial fixing + reduced MILP** (same solver settings as the full MILP): fix 80 / 90 / 95 % of the 1,752 (unit,
   hour) decisions; RACLearn-style baseline = largest |p − 0.5| of the imitation model, no guard; guarded rankings =
   adequacy guard (5 % margin, `uc_fixing.adequacy_guard`) + LP-relaxation guard (`fixpolicy.lp_guard`) on
   (a) imitation confidence, (b) imitation with OFF errors × 10, (c) REINFORCE probabilities; the guarded ranking per
   ratio is chosen on 6 val instances by the lowest mean reduced-MILP cost. Fixings that violate min up/down are
   released (`fixpolicy.release_conflicting_rows`) for every rule.

Pipeline time = LP relaxation for the features + guard LPs + reduced MILP (inference < 0.1 s). Speed-up = full-MILP
time from the test generation run / pipeline time, per instance (median) and as a ratio of mean times; a subset of
full MILPs is re-solved back to back with the reduced ones in the same worker to check the effect of machine load.
**Time-to-quality (TTQ)**: first time the full MILP's incumbent log reaches the reduced solution's cost (censored at
the full MILP's termination time when it never does).

## 4. Results

### 4.1 The benchmark: full MILP on the 40 test instances (300 s, 0.1 %)

| | value |
|---|---|
| termination time, mean / median / min | **154 s / 98 s / 3 s** (CPU 108 s mean; CPU / wall 0.70 under the shared load, 1-min load average 6.7) |
| proved 0.1 % / stopped at 300 s | 70 % / 30 % (final gaps 0.10–0.77 %, mean 0.16 % over all instances) |
| first incumbent within 0.5 % / 0.1 % / equal to the final one | median 28 s / 34 s / 54 s (means 38 s / 74 s / 100 s) |
| LP-relaxation gap | 0.38 % mean |
| served (no shedding / reserve shortfall) | 97.5 % |
| units on (mean per hour) | 13.7 |

Hard instances are low-load days (winter / shoulder months: many near-tied commitment choices); summer days solve in
20–40 s. As in the probe, most of the time goes into the dual bound: the median instance has a solution within 0.5 %
after 28 s.

66.5 % above the LP-relaxation bound with top-1 + block repair); REINFORCE checkpoint step 80 (still improving at the
end of the budget); decoding thresholds 0.3 (imitation) and 0.5 (REINFORCE).

Pipeline time: LP relaxation 1.0 s + inference 0.004 s + block repair 0.014 s + dispatch LP 0.24 s = **1.3 s**
(≈ 120× faster than the full MILP); screening ≈ 4.6 s (≈ 33×). But no one-LP pipeline is near-optimal at 24 hours:
the best served share (REINFORCE + screening, 92.5 % vs the MILP's 97.5 %) costs 4.3 % (median) more than the MILP,
and the cheapest learned decoder (imitation + block repair) is *worse* than simply block-repairing the rounded
relaxation (1.5 % median). The B2 pattern is amplified: REINFORCE buys coverage with ~3 extra units per hour
(+10 % median cost), the imitation model under-commits unless repaired.

## 5. Verdict

* **The benchmark works as intended for termination time**: the 24-hour MILP takes 154 s on average (30 % hit
  the 300 s limit) and a 20× speed-up is measurable. Label-free training needs no full MILP: 0.28 core-h of LP
  relaxations for labels plus ≈ 0.5 core-h of dispatch LPs for REINFORCE, against ≈ 13 core-h for 300 full-MILP
  labels.
* **Against Learning to Fix (0.48 % at 20.8×)**: not reached. At ≈ 0.5 % mean gap the label-free pipelines give
  **≈ 4.7× in termination time** (val-selected guarded rules at 80–95 %; RACLearn-style 80 %: 0.33 % at 3.4×). The
  only rules beyond 20× (REINFORCE probabilities at 95 %: 26×) cost 8.8 % on average (5.3 % median); the unguarded
  RACLearn rule reaches 14× at 95 % but sheds load or misses reserve on 60 % of the instances. Learning to Fix's
  operating point is ~4× faster than our ≈ 0.5 % point and ~10–18× better in gap than our > 20× point.
* **Time-to-quality is the binding limit, not the learning**: HiGHS finds a solution within 0.5 % of its final one
  after a median 28 s; the reduced MILPs still need 10–45 s (the 24-hour network LP and the 0.1 % proof dominate;
  with 85–90 % of the commitment decisions fixed the reduced problem still carries all ~17,000 continuous
  variables of the 24-hour network dispatch). Measured as time to the same quality, fixing gives only
  **1.3–2.0×** (median TTQ speed-up of the val-selected rules and RACLearn 80 %; ≤ 0.5 % target: ~2× when both reach
  it, and the pipelines reach it on only 58–78 % of instances). Large termination-time speed-ups therefore mostly reflect the cost of *proving* optimality,
  which the reduced MILP proves only for its restricted space.
* **Guards are what make aggressive fixing usable**: the adequacy + LP-relaxation guards turn the RACLearn rule's
  138 % mean gap (95 %) into 0.52 % with 95 % served, at the price of releasing ~10 % of the fixings (speed-up falls
  from 14× to 4.8×). Ranking choice matters less than on B2 once the guards are on; at 80 % the plain confidence
  rule was best on test although the asymmetric rule won on 6 val instances.
* **End-to-end (one LP, 1.3 s, ≈ 120×)** is not near-optimal at 24 hours: best served share 92.5 % (MILP 97.5 %) at
  4.3 % median gap (REINFORCE + screening), and no learned decoder beats block-repairing the rounded relaxation
  on cost (75 % served, 1.5 % median). REINFORCE's over-commitment (+3 units per hour) grows with the horizon.
* **What would be needed for LtF-level numbers**: a reduced problem that is cheap to *solve to quality*, not only to
  prove, e.g. fixing plus a time-capped reduced MILP warm-started from the predicted schedule, a better-calibrated
  ranking at 95 % (the REINFORCE model is confident but over-commits; a cost-aware calibration à la Learning to Fix
  needs MILP-priced errors, which the label-free protocol avoids), or a commercial solver where the LP part is less
  dominant.

## 6. Limitations

* **Shared machine.** All timings were taken on 4 cores shared with two other agents and another data-generation
  job (1-minute load average 6–9; a solve got ~0.7 of a core). Wall-clock speed-ups are ratios measured under
  similar load (CPU-time speed-ups are reported too, and six full MILPs were re-solved back to back with their
  reduced MILPs in the same worker); seconds are not comparable with B1/B2, which also used another HiGHS build
  (scipy's 1.12 vs highspy 1.15.1 here). HiGHS ran single-threaded; a commercial solver would solve both the full
  and the reduced MILPs much faster, and the ratio may differ.
* **Reference quality.** With the 300 s limit, 30 % of the test references are not proven to 0.1 % (gaps up to
  0.77 %); reduced MILPs can and sometimes do beat them (negative gaps). A longer limit was not affordable.
* **Small samples, single seed.** 40 test instances; the fixing ranking per ratio was chosen on 6 val instances;
  REINFORCE ran 80 steps (still improving). No val full MILPs: selection was label-free (deviation from the plan,
  §3).
* **Baselines.** The RACLearn-style baseline is the confidence rule applied to the label-free imitation model, not
  RACLearn's MILP-trained MC-dropout GNN. Learning to Fix was not re-implemented on B3; the comparison is with its
  published number on different (EPRI competition) systems and a different solver.
* **Not run**: T = 36 (`make_scenario` stays inside one day), self-training rounds with reduced MILPs, the learned
  error-cost ranking of W3 (it needs MILP optima to price errors).
